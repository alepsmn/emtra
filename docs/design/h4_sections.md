### 6.2. Changes with respect to the design

Building the prototype forced some decisions that the design had left open, and a few changes. Table 52 lists the ones that affect the design; the complete log is kept in the repository.

| Design (H3) | Prototype | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Simulator fitted to recorded EMT data (§5.8, §5.11) | Fully synthetic corridor defined in configuration | Every measure needs a ground truth, which only the simulator provides |
| GCS, BigQuery, dbt and Airflow in the cloud tier (D6) | Local directory with the same partition layout, DuckDB and dbt; jobs launched by command | No managed service is needed to demonstrate the design; going back is a change of adapter and path |
| Edge, fog and cloud as containers on one virtual machine (§5.5) | Infrastructure in containers on a laptop; EMTRA services as processes | Simpler to run and record; tier boundaries are unchanged |
| Closing of a 15-minute band not specified (Annex B.1) | The Ingestor publishes a watermark; the ReliabilityMonitor closes a band when the watermark passes its end plus an allowed lateness | With event time, a band without arrivals would never close and a blocked segment could not be detected |
| c_vh computed per stop (§3.3) | Headways of all stops of a segment are pooled in each band; sample standard deviation | One stop gives about two headways per band: too few to classify |
| Level-of-service limits with gaps between classes (Table 12) | Inclusive upper limits up to B, exclusive from C | Removes the ambiguity between two printed limits |
| On time if 0 ≤ delay ≤ 5 min (§3.3) | On time if −60 s ≤ delay ≤ 5 min | With the literal definition an arrival 2 s ahead of schedule is not on time |

| A bus that leaves the list of a stop is "never fulfilled" after a grace time | A poll in which the list limit of the source may hide the bus is not counted as an absence; grace calibrated on fault-free days | The source shows at most two buses per line and stop, so a bus can leave the list and come back |
| Message identifier unspecified (Annex C.3) | Deterministic identifier derived from the schema and the natural key | Consumers remove duplicates after restarts and replays without extra state |
| Increment 1 at H3, increment 2 at H4 (Table 50) | Increment 1 at H4, increment 2 at H5 | Milestones shifted by one |

### 6.3. How the prototype is built

**One contract, written once.** Messages, topics, tables and shared modules are defined in a single interface document and in one code package that every service imports. A change of contract is made in both and logged.

**Each service is a pure core and a thin adapter.** The core takes events and returns events, with no broker and no database. The same core runs live, connected to MQTT and FogStore, and in batch over files. The validation of Section 7 runs in batch; the demonstration runs live. An acceptance test checks that both give identical results.

**Event time, never wall-clock time.** All domain logic uses the times carried by the messages. This is what allows the simulated world to run 60 times faster than real time and the experiments to run in seconds.

**Reproducible randomness.** Every random stream is derived from a root seed and a name. Two runs with the same seed give the same messages, with the same identifiers. Seeds are split by use: baseline, calibration and validation never share a seed.

**The simulated corridor.** Twelve stops in two segments of six, shared by three lines: 19 and C1 (high frequency, measured by c_vh) and 170 (low frequency, measured by OTP). The simulator publishes a snapshot of arrival estimates per stop every 30 s, as the EMT API would, limited to two buses per line, and stores the true arrivals, the waiting times and the injected faults. The fault catalogue has five types (slowdown, blocked segment, breakdown, dispatch delay, demand surge) and three severities.

**The parameters left open in the design are now set.** Detector limits are calibrated on 20 fault-free baseline days for an in-control average run length of 2,000 bands (Table 53). Everything else is in versioned configuration files.

### 6.4. Demonstration

The demonstration starts from an empty system with one command and runs the corridor from 06:00 to 10:15 at 60 times real time. The first hour is warm-up. At 08:00 a high-severity slowdown starts in segment 1. The dashboard shows the level of service of every line and segment per 15-minute band, the map of the corridor, the indicator over time, the open alerts and the health of the services. The regularity of line 19 in segment 1 degrades, the detectors signal the drift, and an "action required" alert opens at 08:30. The run ends by checking that this alert is stored in FogStore. Two consecutive runs give identical contents.

### 7.2. Validation method

The system is validated in batch against the ground truth of the simulator, on seeds that were not used to calibrate anything. Two experiments cover the first increment:

- **E1, normal operation.** Ten fault-free days. Measures the record (F1) and the indicator (F3).
- **E2, detection.** Twenty fault-free days and 92 runs with one injected fault each. Forty of them are the target population: high-severity slowdown and blocked segment, in both segments, ten seeds each. The other 52 (lower severities and the single-vehicle faults) are reported without a target.

An alert is attributed to a fault if it opens in the segment of the fault or an adjacent one within 60 minutes of its start. The size of a fault is measured independently of the detector, as the change of the true indicator between the run with the fault and the same seed without it.

The targets were set before measuring and have not been changed. Two definitions were corrected after the first run and are declared here: the detection target applies to high-severity segment faults, because lower severities change the true c_vh by less than 0.05; and a never-fulfilled estimate counts as false only if the bus arrives within five minutes of the last estimated time.

### 7.4. Discussion

**The record and the indicator are accurate.** Derived arrivals are within 14.5 s of the true ones; the limit of polling every 30 s is 15 s. The indicator computed by the system differs from the one computed with true arrivals by 0.009 on average, far below the width of a level-of-service class.

**Detection depends on the size of the effect.** In segment 1 a high-severity fault is detected 19 times out of 20. In segment 2 the same faults raise the true c_vh by 0.12 to 0.14, which is inside the natural spread of that segment on a fault-free day (0.36 ± 0.18), and are detected 7 times out of 20. Recall over the whole population is 0.65 and the target is not met.

**The alert arrives three bands after the fault.** The median delay is 45 minutes against a target of 30. A band is 15 minutes, and the detectors need several bands of evidence at the calibrated run length. The delay is the price of the low false-alert rate: one false alert every six weeks per line and direction.

**EWMA and CUSUM are worth their complexity.** At the same in-control run length they detect 26 of the 40 faults; a Shewhart chart detects 15.

**Single-vehicle faults are mostly missed.** A breakdown or a dispatch delay changes the true c_vh by up to 0.33, but for one or two bands; they are detected 5 times out of 24. A per-band indicator detects a sustained degradation of a segment, not an incident of one vehicle. Demand surges have no effect on the true indicator and are, correctly, not detected.

**Never-fulfilled estimates are over-reported under faults.** On fault-free days almost none is emitted. With a fault, and above all downstream of a blocked segment, most of the buses declared never fulfilled do arrive close to their last estimate. The source limit of two buses per line and stop explains part of it and is handled; the rest is open. It does not affect the indicator or the alerts, which use arrivals only. It does affect the accuracy measures of the second increment (F4), which will report fault-free and faulty days separately.

**Limitations of the prototype.** The Ingestor and the ReliabilityMonitor keep their state in memory: after a restart there is a gap in the arrivals of the buses being tracked, never a duplicate, and open alerts are closed. A bus retired at a stop produces one derived arrival at that stop that the ground truth does not record. K1 is 100 % by construction in batch; the end-to-end loss is measured with the full stack at H5.

**What the three unmet KPIs mean for the design.** The detection design works as specified where the disturbance is large and sustained. Two changes would address the rest and are left as future work: a second, faster rule on the headway gap of individual vehicles, and bands shorter than 15 minutes on high-frequency lines.
